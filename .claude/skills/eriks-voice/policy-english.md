# Mode: Policy English (English remissvar, expert opinion, policy brief)

Use for the English-language counterparts of the `academic-swedish` genre: consultation
responses (remissvar) to Swedish or EU inquiries, expert opinions to a government inquiry,
SNS/ESO/IFN-style policy reports, and technical policy briefs written in English. This mode
ports the `academic-swedish` register into English. It is the policy and regulatory
counterpart to `academic-english`, which is reserved for research papers and referee
responses.

Important: this mode is an interpolation between two anchored modes, and it is NOT backed by
a verbatim English-remissvar corpus. Anchor the genre, structure, and collective voice on
`academic-swedish.md`, and the English prose rhythm and idiom on `academic-english.md`. The
excerpts below are illustrative English renderings, not verbatim corpus text. Compose in
English from the source material; do not translate a Swedish remissvar literally, since
Erik's English and Swedish prose differ in rhythm.

## Voice rules

1. **"We" is the default voice.** These are committee outputs or co-authored responses.
   First person singular ("I") does not belong here, in contrast to `academic-english`, where
   "I" is the paper default.
2. **Open with an explicit statement of purpose**: *In this response we comment on [the
   inquiry / proposal] ...* / *The purpose of this study is to ...* / *We recommend that ...*
3. **Sentences are medium-long and nominal**, 25 to 40 words, one main clause plus one or two
   qualifying clauses, with embedded references to law, EU directives, or specific provisions.
   Compound rhythm: *X holds, although Y*; *X is the case, while Y suggests ...*
4. **Passive and nominal constructions are acceptable** here, as part of the register: *the
   regulation is constructed*, *the revenue cap is set*, *the Inspectorate should consider*.
5. **Policy verdicts are stated as collective judgment**: *We consider that ...*, *We
   recommend that ...*, *We agree with the inquiry's conclusion that ...*, *We do not support
   ...*, *We advise against ...*
6. **Numbered or bulleted recommendations are permitted and expected**, particularly at the
   end of each major section. This is the exception to Erik's general avoidance of bullets.
7. **Engage the technical vocabulary of the document under review**, and reproduce its key
   terms accurately.
8. **Cite specific sections or pages** of the document being commented on: *The proposal
   contains formulations that work against the stated aim of technology neutrality (p. 238):
   "..."*
9. **The tone is constructive but firm.** Credit where due (*a commendably balanced
   analysis*), substantive critique where needed (*We consider these to be unfortunate
   formulations*).
10. **Distinguish systematically between short run and long run**: *short-run effects*,
    *long-run incentives*. A recurring analytical distinction.

## English prose register (carried from academic-english)

- **Calibrated hedging only.** *approximately*, *roughly*, *on average*, *consistent with*,
  *likely conservative*, *well approximated by*, *plausibly*, *less precisely estimated*.
  Avoid *perhaps*, *maybe*, *we think*, *seems to*.
- **Quantify with units, always.** Every percentage gets a unit: *7 percent (0.05 SEK/kWh)*,
  *28 percent (17 EUR/MWh)*. Never bare percentages.
- **Acknowledge limitations in-line**, not in a footnote: *In the current setup the estimate
  is therefore likely conservative.*
- **Prefer *Although* or *While* plus a main clause** over sentence-initial *However* as a
  soft hedge.

## Vocabulary signatures

- Collective judgment: *We consider*, *We agree*, *We support*, *We recommend*, *We do not
  support*, *We advise against*.
- Evaluative signature: *It is unfortunate that ...* / *We consider these to be unfortunate
  formulations.*
- Normative frame: *economically motivated / efficient / justified*; *socially efficient*.
- Transitions: *in accordance with*, *with considerable caution*, *against this background*,
  *setting aside*, *we further note that*.
- Problem framing: *A precondition for ...*, *An important question is ...*, *The basic idea
  is that ...*, *It is therefore important that ...*
- Market terminology: *in the absence of competition*, *natural monopoly*, *market-based
  compensation*, *crowd out efficient market solutions*.
- Recurring evaluative anchor: *technology neutrality*, used both as a measure of the
  inquiry's quality and as an independent normative benchmark.

## Things to avoid

- **No em-dashes and no en-dashes.** Use commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, or a full
  stop and a new sentence.
- No rhetorical flourishes, no metaphors, no everyday analogies, no rhetorical questions.
- No first person singular ("I"). Collective "we" throughout.
- No emotionally evaluative adjectives (*striking*, *remarkable*, *shocking*). The strongest
  evaluative is *notable* or *particularly useful*.
- No buzzwords (*novel*, *groundbreaking*, *cutting-edge*).
- No journalistic figures (*SEK 320 per household*). Use structural percentages, öre/kWh or
  EUR/MWh, billion-level amounts, with regulatory context.
- No open speculation. Anchor claims in economic theory, regulatory text, or empirics.
- No ridicule of the inquiry under review. Critique is technical and structured.
- No superlatives. No exclamation points.
- Do not name political actors personally. Use *the government*, *the inquiry*, *the
  Inspectorate*.

## Terminology

A consultation response addresses a Swedish or EU inquiry and engages domestic regulatory
concepts. Keep the key Swedish regulatory terms and gloss them on first use: *intäktsram
(revenue cap)*, *tillsynsperiod (supervision period)*, *förhandsreglering (ex ante
regulation)*, *Energimarknadsinspektionen (the Swedish Energy Markets Inspectorate, Ei)*.
After the first gloss, the English term or the abbreviation may be used. Do not silently
translate a term whose Swedish form carries legal specificity.

## Structural template

### A. Consultation response (remissvar)
1. **Title**: *Response to "[inquiry title]"* plus reference number and date.
2. **Introduction** (1 paragraph): which document is being commented on, a quotation from its
   terms of reference, and who is responding.
3. **General assessment** (1 to 2 paragraphs): the overall judgment. What is well done, and
   where the main critique lies.
4. **Section-by-section comment**: subheadings that follow the chapter structure of the
   document under review (*Chapter 4: Risk Management and Risk Sharing*). Within each:
   identify a specific formulation or argument, quote if necessary, then *We consider that
   ...* with the counterargument or refinement.
5. **Summary of views and recommendations** (numbered or bulleted).
6. **Signature block**: author name with academic degree and institutional affiliation.

### B. Policy report (SNS/ESO/IFN equivalent)
1. **Foreword and summary**: 1 to 2 pages of main conclusions, often as numbered points.
2. **Introduction**: institutional background, problem statement, purpose, data sources.
3. **Framework**: short and to the point.
4. **Analysis**: data, method, results, with figures and tables.
5. **Discussion and policy recommendations**: how the results translate into reform.
6. **Appendices**: technical detail.

## Quick voice check for this mode

Before delivering, confirm:
- [ ] "We" throughout. No first person singular.
- [ ] The opening sentence states explicitly which document is being commented on.
- [ ] At least one piece of constructive credit in the general assessment before the critique
  begins.
- [ ] Technical vocabulary from the document under review reproduced accurately.
- [ ] Swedish regulatory terms glossed on first use.
- [ ] Recommendations numbered or bulleted at the end.
- [ ] Any quotation from the inquiry has a page reference.
- [ ] English hedging is calibrated (approximately, consistent with, likely conservative),
  not soft (perhaps, seems to).
- [ ] No party-political evaluation, no exclamation points, no superlatives, no metaphors, no
  dashes.
