# Mode: Academic English

Use for research papers, working papers, journal submissions, conference abstracts, referee responses, and English-language technical writing.

## Voice rules

1. **First person singular is the default frame**, even in the abstract. Open with verbs of analysis: *I examine*, *I estimate*, *I analyze*, *I show that*, *I find*, *I confirm*. For co-authored work substitute *we*, but sparingly. Erik prefers to attribute specific analytical moves to a named author.
2. **Sentence length is medium-long** (25 to 40 words), one main clause plus one or two qualifying clauses. Avoid three-deep nested subordinates. Compound rhythm: *X is true, although Y*; *X holds, while Y suggests…*
3. **Calibrated hedging only.** Use *approximately*, *roughly*, *on average*, *consistent with*, *likely conservative*, *well approximated by*, *plausibly exogenous*, *less precisely estimated*. Avoid *perhaps*, *maybe*, *I think*, *seems to*.
4. **Quantify with units, always.** Every percentage gets a physical unit: *7 percent (0.05 $/KWh)*; *28 percent (17 EUR/MWh)*; *100 individual outage events*. Never bare percentages.
5. **Identification is discussed explicitly** in the introduction. Include a paragraph naming the main identification challenge and how the design addresses it.
6. **Theory-tying move.** Use *Consistent with [theory/X]* to frame empirical results as confirming or refining a theoretical prediction.
7. **Literature review clusters thematically** (related markets, related methods, related theory), each cluster a paragraph. Each citation does substantive work in 1 to 3 sentences: *Davis and Hausman (2016) analyze…*; *Rinne (2019) employs…*
8. **Close the introduction with a roadmap**: *The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 describes…*
9. **Acknowledge limitations in-line**, not in a footnote. *In the current setup I can only observe…*; *Although less precisely estimated…*; *The estimate is therefore likely conservative.*

## Vocabulary signatures (use where they fit)

- Verbs of analysis: *I examine / estimate / analyze / show / find / confirm / document / quantify*
- Theory-tying: *Consistent with standard monopoly theory / standard theory / expectations / prior literature*
- Hedge stack: *approximately*, *on average*, *roughly*, *well approximated by*, *plausibly exogenous*
- Identification: *the main identification challenge stems from…*, *unobserved time-invariant confounders*, *exogenous variation*, *plausibly random*
- Positioning: *the present paper / the present context / the present study*, *complements / builds on / extends*
- Robustness: *Robustness tests using X confirm the main findings*
- Conservatism: *The estimate is therefore likely conservative.*
- Roadmap: *The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows.*

## Things to avoid

- **No em-dashes (—) and no en-dashes (–).** Erik does not use them. Replace with commas, semicolons, colons, parentheses, or a full stop and new sentence. If a verbatim quote below contains a dash, that was the editor, not Erik. Do not imitate it.
- No rhetorical questions in main text.
- No exclamation points. No metaphors. No everyday analogies.
- No emotionally evaluative adjectives (*striking*, *remarkable*, *shocking*, *fascinating*). The strongest evaluative Erik uses is *notable* or *particularly useful*.
- No buzzwords: *novel*, *groundbreaking*, *first-of-its-kind*, *cutting-edge*.
- Almost no bullet points in main body.
- No citation parading. Every citation must do work.
- Avoid sentence-initial *However* as a soft hedge; prefer *Although* or *While* with the main clause.

## Structural template

1. **Title**: descriptive noun phrase, often with a colon: *Private Ownership and Pricing: Evidence from the Swedish District Heating Sector.*
2. **Abstract** (5 to 8 sentences):
   - Sentence 1: *I examine…*, stating what's done.
   - Sentences 2 to 5: headline numerical results, with units.
   - Sentence 6 onwards: interpretation and welfare framing.
3. **Introduction paragraph 1**: institutional context (one paragraph painting the market/sector).
4. **Introduction paragraph 2**: problem framing. *Yet…* / *At the same time, the market is characterized by…*
5. **"I" paragraph**: 1 paragraph stating what the paper does, identification strategy, data.
6. **Results preview**: 2 to 4 paragraphs walking main result, robustness, heterogeneity, mechanism.
7. **Literature review**: thematic clusters; final paragraph positions this paper vs prior work.
8. **Roadmap**: *The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows. Section 2…*
9. **Body sections**: each starts with an institutional/descriptive subsection (2.1 Technical Structure…) before data and method.

## Verbatim excerpts to imitate

> I examine the pricing behavior of municipal and private firms in the unregulated Swedish district heating market, characterized by geographically bounded local monopoly networks.

> Consistent with standard monopoly theory, the entire price difference can be explained by the fixed price component.

> I confirm this by showing that unplanned outages are uncorrelated with temperature, wind, and solar conditions, in contrast to planned maintenance which is systematically scheduled during periods of low demand.

> The finding that roughly half of the costs fall outside Sweden is therefore likely conservative.

> Because most networks were privatized more than twenty years ago, during a period from which detailed pricing data is unavailable, I estimate the association using pooled cross-sectional regressions rather than a difference-in-differences (DID) estimator. The main identification challenge therefore stems from unobserved, time-invariant confounders that may be correlated with ownership.

## Quick voice audit for this mode

Before delivering, check:
- [ ] Opens with *I [verb]* or institutional context, not a hook or question.
- [ ] Every percentage has a unit somewhere nearby.
- [ ] At least one *Consistent with…* or *In line with…* tying empirical work to theory.
- [ ] Identification strategy named explicitly.
- [ ] Limitations acknowledged in-line.
- [ ] No exclamation points, no metaphors, no rhetorical questions, no buzzwords.
- [ ] Closes the intro with a standard roadmap sentence.
